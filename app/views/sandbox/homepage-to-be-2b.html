What it is like to be an NHS volunteer

There are many reasons why you may decide to volunteer within the NHS.

As an NHS volunteer, you can:

  • make a positive contribution to the experience of patients and their families
  • do something for the benefit of your local community
  • get a sense of accomplishment and a positive outlook on life
  • do something worthwhile and meaningful to you.

Whether you’re retired, studying or considering a change in career, volunteering is a great way to learn new skills and discover new possibilities. You can volunteer in person or take part in some opportunities remotely from home.

You will get support from dedicated teams who welcome volunteers from all walks of life and backgrounds.

How you can help

  • Support your community

    Become an active member of your community and make a positive impact on the health of your neighbours

  • Provide companionship and assist with patient recreation

    Improve the comfort of patients during their stay and lift their spirits through a range of creative activities

  • Support NHS charities and fundraising

    Join NHS charities that help support and fund services for our patients, visitors and staff

  • Assist with admin tasks

    Assist staff with general desk tasks to improve volunteering activities

  • Support the delivery of clinical care

    Contribute to activities that improve the physical and mental health of patients

  • Take part in opportunities from home

    Make a difference from a distance. You may need a phone or a computer and some basic tech skills
